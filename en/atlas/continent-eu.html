Europe

Connected plains, peninsulas and mountain belts

Europe is joined to Asia as one big landmass. It has wide plains in the north and many peninsulas reaching into the sea in the south. It also has tall mountains like the Alps.

Europe is the second-smallest continent, yet its coastline is long and intricate, with many peninsulas and islands. Broad plains, young mountain belts and ice-carved northern lands sit together.

Joined to Asia

Europe and Asia are not separated by sea; they form one landmass called Eurasia. The Ural Mountains and the Caucasus area are usually taken as the boundary.

Source: National Geographic Society, Europe: Physical Geography · Natural Earth, Land, 1:50m

Young mountains in the south

The Alps, Pyrenees and Caucasus rose as the African plate pushed toward Europe. Europe’s highest peak is Mount Elbrus in the Caucasus, at 5,642 m.

Source: National Geographic Society, Europe: Physical Geography · NASA Earth Observatory, Spring Snow Cover in the Alps

The great northern plain

The North European Plain runs from western France to Russia. Most of it lies below 152 m, making it good farmland crossed by many large rivers.

Source: National Geographic Society, Europe: Physical Geography

Regions

Regions

Western Europe

From Atlantic islands to the Alps

Western Europe includes the islands of Britain and Ireland across the sea. The mainland has low, wide plains, and the snowy Alps rise in the south.

The region spans Atlantic islands and coasts, the lowlands of the Rhine and the Alpine highlands to the south. Winds from the ocean keep winters mild for such northern latitudes.

Islands and mainland coasts

Britain and Ireland are islands west of mainland Europe. Shallow seas such as the North Sea and the English Channel separate them from the mainland.

Source: Natural Earth, Land, 1:50m

The Rhine and North Sea lowlands

The Rhine starts in the Alps, crosses Germany and reaches the North Sea in the Netherlands. Much of the low Dutch land lies below sea level and is protected by dikes and canals.

Source: NASA, Space Radar Image of Rhine River, France and Germany

The Alps in the south

The Alps run from southeastern France through Switzerland and Austria. Glaciers carved deep valleys and left many lakes.

Source: NASA Earth Observatory, Spring Snow Cover in the Alps

United Kingdom An island country · Buckingham Palace and red buses. France The country of the Eiffel Tower · baguettes and wine. Germany The country of sausages and beer · Neuschwanstein Castle.

Southern Europe

Mediterranean peninsulas and mountains

Southern Europe has three peninsulas reaching into the Mediterranean. Italy is shaped like a boot. Summers are sunny with very little rain.

The region is made of the Iberian, Italian and Balkan peninsulas and Mediterranean islands. Mountains rise right behind the coasts, and the Mediterranean climate brings dry summers and wet winters.

Three different peninsulas

The Iberian Peninsula in the west is blocky, with a high central plateau. Italy stretches out like a boot, and the Balkan Peninsula to the east is mountainous with many offshore islands.

Source: Natural Earth, Land, 1:50m

Mountains near the sea

The Dolomites in northern Italy have 18 peaks over 3,000 m. The Apennines run down the length of the Italian peninsula.

Source: UNESCO, The Dolomites · National Geographic Society, Europe: Physical Geography

Mediterranean seasons

Mediterranean coasts have hot, dry summers and rainy winters. Plants that cope with summer drought, such as olives and grapes, grow well here.

Source: European Commission JRC, Soil Resources of Mediterranean and Caucasus Countries

Spain The country of flamenco and Gaudí's architecture. Italy A boot-shaped country · pizza and pasta. Greece White houses with blue roofs · the birthplace of the Olympics. Türkiye A country spread across two continents · hot-air balloons and Cappadocia.

Northern Europe

Fjords, lakes and a volcanic island

Long ago, Northern Europe was buried under thick ice. The ice carved deep sea inlets and left countless lakes. Volcanic Iceland is here too.

The region includes the Scandinavian mountains, Norway’s fjords, the forests and lakes of Sweden and Finland, and the volcanic landscapes of Iceland-land shaped by both ice and fire.

Norway’s fjords

A fjord is a long, narrow inlet where the sea has flooded a deep valley carved by a glacier. They are common on Norway’s west coast, with steep cliffs on both sides.

Source: UNESCO, West Norwegian Fjords - Geirangerfjord and Nærøyfjord

Forests, lakes and rising land

Forests and lakes cover much of Sweden and Finland. Freed from the weight of the ice, the land is still rising: Sweden’s High Coast has risen about 285 m since the ice age.

Source: National Geographic Society, Europe: Physical Geography · UNESCO, High Coast / Kvarken Archipelago

Iceland, where the land splits

Iceland sits where the North American and Eurasian plates pull apart. Cracks in the ground, volcanoes and hot springs appear across the island.

Source: UNESCO, Þingvellir National Park

Norway Fjords and the northern lights · the home of the Vikings. Sweden Northern lights and Vikings · the country of the Nobel Prize.

Eastern Europe

Broad plains and their mountain margins

Eastern Europe has a plain so wide you can hardly see its end. The Volga, Europe’s longest river, flows across it.

The region holds the vast East European Plain, the Carpathian and Caucasus mountains on its edges, and the Volga flowing into the Caspian Sea.

The East European Plain

Low, flat land stretches across Belarus, Ukraine and western Russia. Much of it has rich dark soil where wheat and other grains are grown.

Source: National Geographic Society, Europe: Physical Geography

Mountains on the edges

The curved Carpathians lie to the west of the plain and the Caucasus to the southeast. Mount Elbrus in the Caucasus, at 5,642 m, is Europe’s highest peak.

Source: National Geographic Society, Europe: Physical Geography · Natural Earth, Land, 1:50m

The Volga to the Caspian

The Volga, about 3,700 km long, is Europe’s longest river. It empties into the landlocked Caspian Sea rather than an ocean, building a broad delta at its mouth.

Source: NASA Earth Observatory, Volga River Delta · NASA Earth Observatory, Volga Delta and the Caspian Sea

Poland Warsaw's Old Town rebuilt from old paintings · the land of pierogi and apples. Hungary A Parliament on the Danube · the country of paprika and goulash.
