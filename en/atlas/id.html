Indonesia

Indonesia

One of the countries with the most islands in the world · Bali.

Capital JakartaPopulation About 280 millionArea 1,904,569 km²Language Indonesian
Map

Reading the land

Indonesia is a country of many islands strung along the equator. The islands have lots of volcanoes, and the hot, rainy weather keeps the forests thick and green.

Indonesia spreads between the Indian and Pacific Oceans. Large islands such as Sumatra, Java, Kalimantan, Sulawesi and western New Guinea, along with many smaller ones, form volcanic ranges along plate boundaries.

Terrain

Mountain spines and lowland coasts

A long mountain chain runs along Sumatra’s southwest coast. Java has a line of volcanoes, including Mount Semeru at 3,676 m, with low plains along the coasts.

Source: NASA Earth Observatory, Sumatra Subduction Zone · Indonesia Travel, Bromo Tengger Semeru National Park

Each island is different

Kalimantan has forested mountains and low plains crossed by large rivers. Sulawesi is a mountainous island with several long arms, and western New Guinea has high central ranges.

Source: NASA Earth Observatory, Kalimantan, Borneo, Indonesia

Coasts & islands

An island nation across the equator

Indonesia stretches between the Indian and Pacific Oceans. Borneo is shared by Indonesia’s Kalimantan, Malaysia and Brunei, and New Guinea is split between Indonesia in the west and Papua New Guinea in the east.

Source: Natural Earth, Land, 1:50m · NASA Earth Observatory, Kalimantan, Borneo, Indonesia

Islands east of Java

Bali and the Nusa Tenggara islands continue east of Java like a chain, separated by deep water and narrow straits.

Source: BMKG, Tatanan tektonik Nusa Tenggara Timur · Natural Earth, Land, 1:50m

Geology

Volcanoes where plates meet

Off Sumatra and Java, the Indian Ocean plate sinks beneath the Asian plate, building chains of volcanoes and a deep ocean trench. In the eastern islands, several plates meet in more complex ways.

Source: NASA Earth Observatory, Sumatra Subduction Zone · BMKG, Tatanan tektonik Nusa Tenggara Timur

A sea of volcanic sand

Around Mount Bromo in East Java lies a “sea of sand” covering about 5,250 hectares. It formed when volcanic ash and sand filled a huge hollow left by a great eruption.

Source: Indonesia Travel, Bromo Tengger Semeru National Park

City

Jakarta

Jakarta is the biggest city in Indonesia. The Monas tower has a golden flame on its top. The tower celebrates the country's independence.

Jakarta is a large city on the northwest coast of Java and plays a major role in Indonesia’s administration and economy. The Monas monument in Merdeka Square has a golden flame at its top and commemorates independence. Modern towers, roads and tropical trees shape the city, while Kota Tua preserves buildings from the Batavia period, when the Dutch ruled the city. Indonesia is developing Nusantara in Kalimantan to relocate government functions.

Heritage

Borobudur

Borobudur is a huge Buddhist temple more than 1,000 years old. You climb its stone levels by stairs. At the top, bell-shaped stone stupas stand in rows.

Borobudur is a Buddhist monument in the Kedu Valley of central Java, built in the eighth and ninth centuries. Broad square terraces support circular platforms and a large central stupa. Rows of perforated bell-shaped stupas stand on the upper platforms, while finely carved reliefs decorate the lower walls. Stairways connect the levels. Major restoration in the twentieth century involved Indonesia and UNESCO, and the temple is preserved as a World Heritage site.

Nature

Mount Bromo

Bromo is a volcano that still puffs out smoke. Around it lies a wide, sandy plain covered with volcanic ash. The view is especially beautiful at sunrise.

Bromo is an active volcano in the Tengger volcanic landscape of eastern Java. Ash and sand cover the broad caldera floor, and Bromo’s crater contrasts with neighboring Mount Batok. Eruptions and erosion shape the craters, fluted slopes and surrounding plain.

Plants

Rafflesia

Rafflesia is the biggest flower in the world. When it opens, it can be 1 m across. It smells bad on purpose to bring flies that carry its pollen.

The rafflesia is an unusual flower you can see in the rainforests of Sumatra and Borneo in Indonesia. It is the largest single flower in the world, reaching up to 1 m across. With no leaves or stem, it lives by parasitism on a host plant and rarely blooms. It is a mysterious flower that draws flies with a strong smell to help with pollination.

Culture

Kebaya

The kebaya is a traditional blouse that closes at the front. Its thin cloth has delicate flower embroidery. It is worn with a batik-patterned skirt.

The kebaya is a front-opening traditional blouse worn in Indonesia and other parts of Southeast Asia. Fine fabric may carry intricate embroidery or lace, with brooches or buttons used as fasteners. In Indonesia it is often paired with a batik kain or sarong. Colors and forms vary by region and occasion. In 2024, knowledge, skills and traditions related to the kebaya were inscribed by UNESCO through a joint nomination from Indonesia and four other countries.

Food

Nasi Goreng

Nasi goreng means "fried rice." It is fried with sweet soy sauce, so it tastes sweet and salty. It is topped with a fried egg and served with crunchy shrimp crackers.

Nasi goreng, which means "fried rice," is Indonesia's best-known fried rice. It is stir-fried with a sweet soy sauce called kecap manis, giving it a deep color and a sweet-and-salty taste. Because it can easily be made with leftover rice, many people also eat it for breakfast. A fried egg is placed on top, and it is served with shrimp crackers called krupuk along with cucumber and tomato. In Indonesia, made up of more than 17,000 islands, the ingredients differ a little from region to region.

Animals

Komodo Dragon

The Komodo dragon is the biggest lizard in the world. It can grow up to 3 m long, longer than a grown-up is tall. It flicks its long tongue to smell food far away.

The Komodo dragon is the largest lizard in the world, growing more than 3 m long and over 70 kg. It lives only on the dry grasslands of Komodo Island and a few islands around it in Indonesia. By flicking its long tongue, it reads smells in the air to find food even a few kilometers away. It hunts deer and wild boar, and scientists were surprised to learn that its saliva contains venom. Because it lives in such a small area, it is protected as an endangered species, and its habitat is set aside as Komodo National Park.

Greeting

Selamat pagi

Selamat pagi means "good morning." Different greetings are used in the afternoon and at night. Indonesian is written with the same alphabet as English.

Selamat pagi means “Good morning” in Indonesian and is a polite greeting used in the morning. Other expressions suit other times, including Selamat siang around midday and Selamat malam at night. Indonesian is the official language connecting diverse island and language communities, and it is written in the Latin alphabet.

Flag

Indonesia's Flag

Indonesia's flag is red on top and white on the bottom. It is a very simple flag with just two colors. Red stands for courage and white for a pure heart.

Indonesia's flag is made of just two colors - red on top and white on the bottom. It is called "Sang Saka Merah Putih" (the red-and-white flag). Officially, red stands for courage and white for purity. It is said to carry on from the red-and-white flag used by the Majapahit kingdom about 700 years ago. It has the same colors as Monaco's flag, but you can tell them apart because their width-to-height ratios differ.
