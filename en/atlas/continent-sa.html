South America

Andes, river basins and eastern highlands

The Andes, the world’s longest mountain range, run down the west of South America. In the middle is the Amazon, the largest rainforest on Earth.

The western Andes, the central Amazon Basin, eastern highlands and the southern grasslands and Patagonia make very different landscapes.

The Andes in the west

The Andes run more than 7,000 km along the west of the continent. The highest peak, Aconcagua, is 6,962 m, and the range holds a high plateau, the Altiplano, at about 3,700 m.

Source: National Geographic Society, South America: Physical Geography

The Amazon and its rainforest

The Amazon basin covers almost 7 million km². Its many tributaries carry water east to the Atlantic, and the forest holds about half of Earth’s rainforest.

Source: National Geographic Society, South America: Physical Geography · NASA Earth Observatory, Amazon River in the Atlantic Ocean

Eastern highlands

The Brazilian Highlands lie south of the Amazon Basin and the Guiana Highlands to its north. The Brazilian Highlands average about 1,000 m.

Source: National Geographic Society, South America: Physical Geography

South America

Forests, grasslands and drylands within one continent

South America has rainforests with lots of rain and deserts where it almost never rains. Windy Patagonia lies at the southern tip.

The humid Amazon forest, the Atacama Desert on the west coast, the Pampas grasslands in the southeast and Patagonia in the south differ greatly in terrain and rainfall.

Amazon forest and rivers

The Amazon Basin holds rainforest and countless tributaries. The Amazon pours more than 200,000 m³ of fresh water into the Atlantic every second.

Source: NASA Earth Observatory, Amazon River in the Atlantic Ocean · National Geographic Society, South America: Physical Geography

Atacama and Pampas

The Atacama Desert, west of the Andes, gets only about 1 mm of rain a year. In contrast, the Pampas of Argentina and Uruguay in the southeast are broad grasslands.

Source: National Geographic Society, South America: Physical Geography

Western and eastern Patagonia

In Patagonia, the western side of the Andes is wet, with glaciers and forests. The mountains block moist winds, leaving dry plateaus and steppe to the east.

Source: NASA Earth Observatory, Snow in the Shadow of the Andes
