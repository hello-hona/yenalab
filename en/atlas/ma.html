Morocco

المغرب

The gateway to the Sahara · the blue city of Chefchaouen.

Capital RabatPopulation About 37 millionArea 446,550 km²Language Arabic and Berber
Map

Reading the land

The high Atlas Mountains cross Morocco at a slant through the middle. At its northern tip, Morocco faces Europe across a narrow strip of sea.

In Morocco the Atlas Mountains stretch from southwest to northeast behind the low Atlantic plains, and the Rif mountains rise along the northern Mediterranean coast. At the northern tip, the Strait of Gibraltar links the Atlantic and the Mediterranean.

Terrain

The High Atlas

The High Atlas begins near Agadir on the Atlantic coast and runs hundreds of kilometres northeast inland. Its tallest peaks are in the western part, and some rise above 4,000 m.

Source: NASA Earth Observatory, Morocco's High Atlas Mountains

The Rif in the north

The Rif mountains curve like a bow along the northern Mediterranean coast. Together with a range in southern Spain across the sea, they form one great bow-shaped mountain system, the Gibraltar Arc.

Source: NASA Earth Observatory, The Strait of Gibraltar

Seas

The Strait of Gibraltar joins two seas

The Strait of Gibraltar at Morocco's northern tip is a narrow seaway joining the Atlantic Ocean and the Mediterranean Sea. At its narrowest it is only 13 km wide, so Africa and Europe face each other very closely.

Source: NASA Earth Observatory, The Strait of Gibraltar

The Mediterranean that dried and refilled

About 6 million years ago, the land here rose and cut the Mediterranean off from the Atlantic, and the sea almost dried up. About 300,000 years later, Atlantic water poured back in and filled the Mediterranean again.

Source: NASA Earth Observatory, The Strait of Gibraltar

Geology

Mountains that began as cracked land

About 201 to 174 million years ago, as a great continent broke apart, the land here stretched and split, forming a valley that the sea flooded. The blocks of land left standing high at that time became the base of today's High Atlas.

Source: NASA Earth Observatory, Morocco's High Atlas Mountains

Mountains pushed up by two continents

From about 66 million years ago to today, the African and Eurasian plates have pushed against each other, lifting the old split blocks of land even higher. That is why the Atlas Mountains have many faults, breaks in the rock.

Source: NASA Earth Observatory, Morocco's High Atlas Mountains

City

Rabat

Rabat is the capital of Morocco. It is a city by the sea. An old walled quarter and a newer city sit side by side.

Rabat is the capital of Morocco, on the Atlantic coast. A settlement existed from Roman times, and the city's name comes from a military base (ribat) developed by the Almohad dynasty in the 12th century. In the early 20th century, a new town was planned next to the traditional medina, forming an unusual scene where traditional and modern city layouts stand together. It became a UNESCO World Cultural Heritage site in 2012.

Heritage

Ait Ben Haddou

Ait Ben Haddou is an old fortress village built of mud bricks. Long ago, traders crossing the desert stopped to rest there. Because it is made of earth, it needs repairs often.

Ait Ben Haddou is a mud-brick fortress village at the foot of the Atlas Mountains in southern Morocco. It was a stop on a trade route for salt and gold that crossed the Sahara to Marrakesh, and most of the buildings standing today were built from the 17th century on. The whole village is built with the pisé method, using local clay and straw, and must be repaired regularly with the same materials. A leading example of Berber group-defense building, it became a UNESCO World Cultural Heritage site in 1987.

Nature

Sahara Desert

The Sahara is the biggest hot desert in the world. In southern Morocco there are sand dunes up to 150 m high. You can travel through the desert on a camel.

The Sahara Desert is the largest hot desert in the world, covering northern Africa. Near Merzouga in southern Morocco, a field of sand dunes up to 150 m high spreads out, and the red of the sand comes from iron oxide coating the grains. Rock carvings of animals around the area show that long ago this land was a wetter grassland than it is today. The night skies are clear and full of stars, and camel trekking and tent camping are famous.

Plants

Argan Tree

The argan tree grows well even in dry land. Precious oil is pressed from the seeds in its fruit. The oil is used in cooking and in skin care.

The argan tree is a special tree that grows mainly in the dry areas of southwestern Morocco. It can live for more than 200 years and grows well even in harsh surroundings. Argan oil, taken from the fruit's seeds, is a fine oil used in cooking and cosmetics, and it is deeply tied to Morocco's economy and the lives of Berber women. The area is also a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve.

Culture

Djellaba & Fez

The djellaba is a long, loose robe with a pointed hood. It keeps out hot sun, sandy wind and cold. It is sometimes worn with a red, round hat called a fez.

The djellaba is a long, loose hooded robe worn by Moroccans. Its pointed hood keeps off the desert sun and sandy wind as well as the cold of the mountains. The fez is a red cylinder-shaped hat, said to take its name from the Moroccan city of Fez. A thin band of embroidery is set along the edges of the clothing for decoration. It is worn in the alleys of markets more than 1,000 years old, and at holidays and weddings alike.

Food

Tagine

Tagine is a dish cooked in a pot with a cone-shaped lid. Steam circles inside the lid, so food cooks moist with almost no water. People tear off flatbread and dip it in the sauce.

Tagine is the name for both an earthenware pot with a cone-shaped lid and the Moroccan stew made in it. Steam swirls around inside the tall lid and falls back as drops, so the ingredients cook moist even with almost no water added. Meat and vegetables are cooked slowly for one or two hours with spices like cumin, saffron, and cinnamon. Olives and salt-preserved lemon are added for the special tangy, deep Moroccan flavor. Tearing flat bread by hand and dipping it in the sauce is the Moroccan way.

Animals

Barbary Macaque

The Barbary macaque is a brown monkey with almost no tail. It lives even in snowy mountains. Father monkeys sometimes carry and care for the babies.

The Barbary macaque is a brown monkey with almost no tail, living in the cedar forests of Morocco's Atlas Mountains. It is tough against the cold, able to bear snowy mountains 2,000 m high. It lives in troops of dozens, eating leaves, fruit, and insects. It draws the notice of scholars for the rare behavior of males carrying and caring for the young. As forests shrink, it has become an endangered species, and Morocco protects it by making national parks.

Greeting

As-salamu alaykum

Assalamu alaykum means "peace be with you." The reply is "wa alaykum assalam." In Morocco people use both Arabic and Berber.

"As-salamu alaykum" is read "as-sa-la-mu a-lai-kum" and means "may peace be upon you" in Arabic. To answer, you turn the words around and say "wa alaykum as-salam." In Morocco, along with Arabic, the Indigenous Berber (Amazigh) language is also an official language. Berber is written in the special Tifinagh script, which looks like geometric patterns. Moroccans often mix in French as well, so one person may go back and forth among three languages.

Flag

Morocco's Flag

Morocco's flag has a green star on a red background. The star is drawn in one unbroken line. It is an easy flag to remember, with just two colors.

Morocco's flag has a green five-pointed star in the center of a red field. It is a five-point star whose lines connect in a single unbroken stroke, called the "Seal of Solomon." Red is a color long used by the Moroccan royal family, and green is a color treasured in Islam. The present look - a green star added to a red flag - was set in 1915. It is an easy-to-remember flag made of just two colors.
