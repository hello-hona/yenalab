Ghana

Ghana

The land of cacao and kente cloth · a flag with a black star.

Capital AccraPopulation About 31 millionArea 238,537 km²Language English
Map

Reading the land

Southern Ghana is thick forest, while the north is savanna, grassland with scattered trees. In the middle lies the huge Lake Volta, made when people dammed a river.

In Ghana, rain grows scarcer the farther north you go from the Gulf of Guinea coast, so tropical forest in the southwest gives way to savanna in the north. Lake Volta and the Kwahu Plateau on its southern edge lie in the middle, and a low mountain chain runs along the eastern border with Togo.

Terrain

Forest in the south, savanna in the north

About a third of Ghana, in the south, is covered by evergreen forest and forest that drops its leaves in the dry season, and the rest is savanna with scattered trees. The southwest gets close to 2,000 mm of rain a year, while the far north gets about 1,000 mm.

Source: FAO, Forestry country profile: Ghana

The eastern hills and the highest peak

The Akwapim-Togo Ranges run from southwest to northeast along the eastern border with Togo. Among them, Afadjato (885 m) is known as the highest mountain in Ghana.

Source: Ghana Tourism Authority, Mountain Afadja (Afadjato)

Water & lakes

Lake Volta, made by a dam

When the Akosombo Dam was built across the Volta River in 1965, the water backed up through the valleys and formed Lake Volta. It covers about 8,500 km², one of the largest lakes ever made by people.

Source: International Lake Environment Committee, Volta Lake

Lake Bosomtwi, a round lake in the forest

Lake Bosomtwi in the Ashanti region of the south fills a round hollow in the land. It covers about 49 km² and is more than 70 m deep in places.

Source: Ghana Tourism Authority, Lake Bosomtwi

Geology

The sandstone cliffs of the Kwahu Plateau

The Kwahu Plateau on the southern edge of Lake Volta is made of sandstone, sand that settled in a wide basin long ago and hardened into rock. The sandstone layers are 250 to 300 m thick, and because they tilt, one side is a gentle slope and the other a steep cliff.

Source: British Geological Survey, Voltaian Basin workshop excursion guide

A hollow made by a meteorite

The round hollow that holds Lake Bosomtwi is thought to have been made by a large meteorite about a million years ago. The lake and the forests and hills around it became a UNESCO biosphere reserve in 2016.

Source: Ghana Tourism Authority, Lake Bosomtwi · UNESCO, New biosphere reserves 2016: Lake Bosomtwe (MAB International Coordinating Council)

City

Accra

Accra is the capital of Ghana. In Independence Square stands the Black Star Gate, a white gateway topped with a big black star. More people live there than anywhere else in Ghana.

Accra is the capital of Ghana, on the southern coast facing the Gulf of Guinea. It began as a seaside village where the Ga people fished and traded, and it grew into a city as trade by sea became busy. It became the capital when Ghana became independent in 1957, and in Independence Square by the sea stands the Black Star Gate, built to mark independence. This white stone gateway topped with a large black star was completed together with the square in 1961. In 2021 about 5.45 million people lived in the Greater Accra Region, which includes Accra, making it the most populated region in Ghana.

Heritage

Asante Traditional Buildings

The Asante Traditional Buildings are old buildings of the Asante people, made of mud and wood. Spirals and animal patterns stand out on their mud walls. They are protected as World Heritage.

The Asante Traditional Buildings are old shrine buildings of the Asante people that remain in villages northeast of Kumasi in southern Ghana. They are the last buildings that show the Asante Kingdom, which flourished in the 18th century, and they were made with frames of wood and bamboo plastered with mud to form the walls. Four buildings stand around a courtyard, and they were originally topped with steep thatched roofs. Spirals, vines, animals, birds and plants are carved in raised patterns on the mud walls, and these patterns are linked to Adinkra symbols, each with its own meaning. They were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1980.

Nature

Kakum National Park

Kakum National Park is a thick tropical forest in southern Ghana. Swaying bridges link the tops of tall trees. People can walk along them and see birds and monkeys in the forest.

Kakum National Park is a tropical forest about 30 km inland from Ghana's southern coast. Warm all year round with plenty of rain, it stretches from evergreen rainforest to forest that drops its leaves in the dry season. It covers about 360 km², and seven swaying bridges, together about 350 m long, link big trees at the height of the forest roof in what is called the canopy walkway. African forest elephants, monkeys, and many birds and butterflies live in the forest. Walking along the bridges, people can see up close the life in the treetops that is hard to see from the ground.

Plants

Cacao Tree

The cacao tree grows flowers and pods right on its thick trunk. The seeds inside the pods are dried to make chocolate. Ghana grows a great deal of cacao.

The cacao tree is an evergreen tree widely grown in the forest belt of southern Ghana. It comes from the tropical Americas and grows well in hot, damp places like the shade of big trees. Small pinkish-white flowers bloom not only on its branches but right on its thick trunk, and there rugby-ball-shaped pods form and ripen yellow or red. When the seeds, wrapped in white pulp inside the pod, are fermented and dried, they become cocoa beans, the raw material of chocolate. Tetteh Quarshie is said to have brought seeds to Ghana and planted them in 1879, and today Ghana produces more cocoa than any country except Côte d'Ivoire.

Culture

Kente Cloth

Kente is a bright cloth from Ghana. Many narrow, long strips are woven and sewn side by side. People wrap it around themselves for festivals and graduations.

Kente is a bright cloth woven by the Asante and Ewe peoples of Ghana. Long strips about 10 cm wide are woven on a narrow loom, and then many strips are sewn side by side to make a large cloth. The geometric patterns, woven with threads of gold, green, red, blue and other colors, each have a name and a meaning. In the Asante village of Bonwire, a story tells of two men who learned to weave by watching a spider spin its web. It was once the cloth the Asante king wore on great occasions, and today many people wrap themselves in it for graduations, weddings and festivals.

Food

Fufu

Fufu is a stretchy food made by pounding cassava and plantain in a mortar. It is shaped into a ball and served in soup. People pinch off pieces by hand and dip them in the soup.

Fufu is a soft, stretchy Ghanaian dough made by pounding boiled cassava and plantain, a kind of cooking banana, in a wooden mortar. One person pounds with a long wooden pestle while another turns the dough by hand and adds a little water at a time, making it smooth and springy. The round balls of fufu are served in soup: light soup made with tomato and pepper, groundnut soup, or palm nut soup. The traditional way to eat it is to pinch off small pieces by hand and dip them in the soup. Today it is sometimes pounded by machine, but many people say it tastes best pounded in a mortar.

Animals

African Grey Parrot

The African grey parrot is a parrot with an ash-gray body and a red tail. It is very clever and good at copying sounds. Its forests have shrunk, so it needs protection now.

The African grey parrot is a parrot about 33 cm long that lives in the tropical forests of southern Ghana. Its body is covered in ash-gray feathers, only its tail is bright red, and it has bare white skin around its eyes. It flies in flocks, eats oil palm fruit, seeds and other fruit, and nests in holes in big trees. It is famous for being clever and very good at copying many kinds of sounds. But as forests shrank and many birds were caught, its numbers in Ghana fell by more than 90% in about 20 years, and it is now protected as an endangered species.

Greeting

Akwaaba!

Akwaaba means "welcome" in Ghana. People say it to greet guests warmly. It is Twi, the most widely spoken local language in Ghana.

Akwaaba, read "ah-kwah-bah," is a Twi word that means "welcome." People say it warmly when guests come to visit, when travelers land at the airport, or when family members come home after work. Twi is a variety of the Akan language and is the most widely spoken local language in Ghana. Akan is a tonal language, where the rise and fall of the voice changes a word's meaning. Ghana's official language is English, but in daily life people also speak Twi and many other local languages.

Flag

Ghana's Flag

Ghana's flag has three stripes: red, gold and green. There is a black star in the middle. It was first raised on the day of independence in 1957.

Ghana's flag has three horizontal stripes, red, gold and green from the top, with a black star on the middle gold stripe. It was designed by Theodosia Okoh for a contest and first raised on March 6, 1957, the day Ghana became independent. Okoh explained that red stands for the people who worked for independence, gold for the country's resources, and green for its forests and rich land. The black star stands for the freedom and unity of African people. Ghana's national football team takes its name from this star and is called the Black Stars.
