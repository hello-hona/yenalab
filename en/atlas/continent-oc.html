Oceania

Large landmasses and varied Pacific islands

Oceania is made of the big land of Australia and thousands of Pacific islands. The world’s largest coral reef is here too.

Australia, New Zealand, New Guinea and the Pacific island groups differ in landscape and origin. By land area, Oceania is the smallest continent.

Australia’s broad, flat land

Australia divides into old western plateaus, low central basins and eastern highlands. Even its highest peak, Mount Kosciuszko, is only 2,228 m, making it low and flat for a continent.

Source: Geoscience Australia, Australian Landforms and their History · National Geographic Society, Australia and Oceania: Physical Geography

New Zealand’s Southern Alps

The Southern Alps run along New Zealand’s South Island. They rise along the boundary where two plates collide, and hold glaciers and deep valleys.

Source: New Zealand DOC, Arthur’s Pass National Park · Natural Earth, Land, 1:50m

High volcanic and low coral islands

The Pacific has high islands built by volcanoes and low ring-shaped coral islands called atolls. The Great Barrier Reef off eastern Australia stretches more than 2,000 km and is the world’s largest reef system.

Source: USGS, Islands · NASA Earth Observatory, Great Barrier Reef

Australasia

A dry, wide land and snowy mountain islands

Australia has a wide, dry middle. Across the sea, New Zealand has tall snowy mountains and glaciers.

This region covers Australia and New Zealand: Australia’s broad, dry interior differs sharply from New Zealand’s narrow, high mountains.

Western and central Australia

Western Australia is a broad plateau of very ancient rock, with low basins and deserts in the centre. With so little rain, many rivers never reach the sea and end in salt lakes.

Source: Geoscience Australia, Australian Landforms and their History

The Great Dividing Range

The Great Dividing Range in eastern Australia separates rivers that run to the coast from those that run inland. The inland Murray-Darling basin covers about one million km².

Source: Geoscience Australia, Australian Landforms and their History · National Geographic Society, Australia and Oceania: Physical Geography

Mountains and valleys of the South Island

The Southern Alps of New Zealand’s South Island have deep glacier-carved valleys and braided rivers that split into many channels over gravel.

Source: New Zealand DOC, Arthur’s Pass National Park
